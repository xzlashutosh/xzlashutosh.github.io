Case study / ANALOG DESIGN

10-bit potentiometric
DAC.

A hierarchical resistor-string digital-to-analog converter designed for a 3.3 V analog supply and evaluated before and after layout.

ROLE: RESEARCH INTERNDATE: JUL — SEP 2020TECHNOLOGY: OSU 180 NMEDA: eSIM · NGSPICE · MAGIC

At a glance

As a research intern, I designed a 10-bit potentiometric DAC to specifications provided by VSD Corporation. The resistor-string converter targets a 0–3.3 V output range with an external reference and uses a hierarchical structure to manage the growing circuit size.

10-bit DACResistor-string architectureeSimngSpiceMagicOSU 180 nm

Hierarchical architecture

The DAC is assembled from switch blocks and smaller DAC subcircuits, building up through 2-bit to 9-bit structures before forming the 10-bit design. This keeps the design modular while routing the selected reference level to the output.

Simulation and layout

I simulated the circuit in ngSpice using the eSim design flow and created layout in Magic. The repository includes output-versus-code, integral non-linearity, and differential non-linearity results for pre-layout and post-layout designs.

The design uses an open-source OSU 180 nm library. The project also documents a PDK limitation encountered when modeling the capacitor and the technology-file adjustment used for simulation.

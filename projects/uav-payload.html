Case study / EMBEDDED SYSTEMS

Modular UAV
payload platform.

Electronics and firmware for a modular airborne payload system — from board design and real-time control through production and flight integration.

ROLE: EMBEDDED ELECTRONICS ENGINEERDATE: DEC 2025 — PRESENTDISCIPLINES: HARDWARE · FIRMWARE · SYSTEMS

At a glance

The platform lets a host drone carry interchangeable mission modules. My work has spanned the BASE interface, payload electronics, gimbal control, module communication, and manufacturing hand-off.

KiCadSTM32 · PIC · RP2040UART · CAN · SPIControl systemsDFM

Selected outcomes: lowered PCB fabrication cost by about 30% on the dropper control board, shortened the quoted lead time from 14 to 7 days, and assembled and tested more than 35 BASE units.

The challenge

A modular payload system has to make several independent pieces behave like one product. Power, data, mechanical interfaces, module detection, and drone telemetry all need to work together in a compact airborne setup.

My contribution

  • Designed and iterated multi-layer boards, schematics, power circuits, and manufacturing data in KiCad.
  • Developed bare-metal firmware across PIC, STM32, and RP2040 platforms, including interrupt-driven serial communication and non-blocking state machines.
  • Integrated gimbal feedback, drone attitude data, actuators, LEDs, and temperature sensing.
  • Debugged hardware on the bench with oscilloscopes, electronic loads, and power supplies, then supported assembly, verification, and production release.

Engineering decisions

Timing and manufacturability repeatedly shaped the design. I moved communication out of blocking loops so the modules could keep receiving messages while controlling motors. I also adjusted PCB design rules and layout choices to better fit fabrication capabilities and reduce cost and lead time.

On the gimbal, encoder selection was a system decision: feedback precision mattered, but so did magnetic interference, mechanical fit, communication reliability, and integration with the stabilization loop.

Outcome & next steps

Several modules progressed from prototype electronics and firmware into assembly and manufacturing workflows. The work continues across system integration, sensor selection, production readiness, and field validation.

For future iterations, I would keep improving the test fixtures and automated checks so each module can be verified earlier and more consistently.

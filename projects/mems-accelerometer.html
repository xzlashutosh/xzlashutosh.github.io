Case study / MEMS & MIXED SIGNAL

MEMS
accelerometer.

A single-axis capacitive accelerometer design study for range-of-motion monitoring, developed across mechanical modeling, analog readout, and digital processing.

ROLE: DESIGN & SIMULATIONDATE: FEB — DEC 2024TEAM PROJECT

At a glance

The project explored a capacitive MEMS accelerometer for tracking range of motion during rehabilitation. A moving proof mass changes the differential capacitance between sensing electrodes. The repository describes a target measurement range of −4 g to +4 g and a minimum bandwidth of 5 kHz.

MEMSANSYS WorkbenchTwin BuilderCadence VirtuosoLTspiceVHDL

Device and mechanical modeling

I modeled the mechanical and electrostatic response in ANSYS Workbench, including deflection, modal behavior, and linear perturbation. The design study describes a polysilicon proof mass with differential sensing fingers and a 1 μm electrode gap. Reduced-order modeling connected the device analysis to a system-level model in ANSYS Twin Builder.

Capacitance readout

The analog path converts the differential capacitance change into a voltage, then amplifies and conditions the signal. I worked through a capacitance-to-voltage circuit in LTspice and amplifier design and simulation in Cadence Virtuoso.

Digital processing

The repository includes a digital design that uses a voltage-controlled oscillator to encode the analog output as frequency, then uses FPGA logic written in VHDL to measure frequency and calculate acceleration.

Simulation and validation

The work links mechanical finite-element analysis, reduced-order modeling, circuit simulation, and digital processing. The repository lists physical fabrication and experimental validation as future work, so the reported performance is simulation based rather than a measured device result.

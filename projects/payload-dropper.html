Case study / EMBEDDED CONTROL

Position-feedback
payload release.

Replaced a servo-based approach with a feedback-controlled linear actuator and made the firmware responsive while the mechanism moves.

ROLE: FIRMWARE · PCB · TESTDATE: JAN — SEP 2026PLATFORM: PIC16F15356

At a glance

The dropper releases a payload on command. The module evolved from PWM servo control to a PQ12 linear actuator with potentiometer feedback, driven by an H-bridge.

Embedded C / XC8PIC16F15356DRV8210UARTKiCad

The problem

Software-generated servo timing was competing with the serial protocol. While the processor was busy maintaining PWM, incoming frames could be missed. Open-loop actuation also made it harder to confirm that the mechanism had reached its intended position.

The approach

I moved to a potentiometer-feedback actuator and a non-blocking state machine. The controller reads position, applies a deadband, checks for timeouts, and verifies the final position after settling.

Communication moved to an interrupt-driven UART routine with FIFO buffers. I used COBS framing and CRC16 validation, then added an acknowledgement path so commands could be accepted or denied explicitly.

Board & manufacturing

I designed the Dropper V5 control PCB and adjusted track width, clearance, and annular ring rules to align with the fabricator’s more economical process. That change reduced PCB cost by roughly 30% and the quoted lead time from 14 to 7 days.

Outcome & learning

Assembled and tested 30 dropper modules against the BASE interface, with additional motor and connector verification. This project reinforced that robust real-time behavior depends on designing the control loop and communication path together.

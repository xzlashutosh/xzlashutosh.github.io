NOTE / 01

FIRMWARE · REAL-TIME SYSTEMS

When control timing collides with communication

A controller can be electrically correct and still miss messages if its timing is wrong.

On a payload release module, software-driven servo timing occupied the processor often enough to interfere with serial communication. The practical problem was bigger than the actuator: commands and status messages had to arrive predictably while the mechanism was moving.

I moved communication into an interrupt-driven routine with FIFO buffers and changed the motion logic to a non-blocking state machine. That gave the controller room to keep receiving frames, check actuator position, and confirm commands without waiting for each action to finish.

The broader lesson for me was to treat timing as part of the interface design. A useful control loop must leave predictable time for sensing and communication too.

NOTE / 02

SENSORS · SYSTEM INTEGRATION

Choosing a sensor for the whole system

The best sensor on a datasheet may not be the best sensor inside the product.

While developing closed-loop position feedback for a gimbal, magnetic encoders were convenient to evaluate and integrate. But the surrounding system mattered: a nearby magnet and wireless hardware could interfere with the drone’s heading reference.

That changed the selection problem. I compared encoder approaches against the mechanical envelope, air gap, integration effort, signal margin, and the effect on nearby sensors. Inductive sensing became a strong candidate because it avoids relying on a permanent magnet at the measurement point.

It was a reminder to compare components in context. Resolution is one number; the product has to work as a system.
